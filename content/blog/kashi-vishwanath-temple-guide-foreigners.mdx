# Kashi Vishwanath Temple for Foreigners: A Local's Practical Guide

Kashi Vishwanath is the heart of Varanasi. For Hindus, it is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas — the most sacred shrines of Lord Shiva — and Kashi itself is understood as Shiva's city. When a Banarasi says they are "going to Baba," this is the temple they mean.

I was born in the galis (lanes) of Kashi, and I still remember the old approach to the temple through lanes so narrow two people couldn't pass. Today there's a large, open corridor and a modern security system. It's much easier to visit — but there are rules, and foreigners often get confused at the gate.

Here's exactly what to expect.

## Can Foreigners Enter Kashi Vishwanath?

**Yes.** Non-Hindus and foreign visitors are allowed to enter Kashi Vishwanath Temple and have darshan (a viewing of the deity). You don't need to be Hindu or to perform any ritual. You're simply expected to be respectful.

Historically, foreign visitors have been directed to a specific gate — usually in the Gate 2 area on the Godowlia side — and asked to show their original passport. The procedure has changed more than once over the years. **Carry your original passport, and ask at the official help desk near the entrance which gate to use on the day.** Don't rely on anyone in the lane outside to tell you.

## Timings

The temple usually opens around 2:30 to 3 AM and closes around 11 PM, with short breaks for the aartis. Approximate aarti times:

- **Mangala Aarti:** around 3 to 4 AM. This is ticketed and very popular.
- **Bhog Aarti:** around midday.
- **Sandhya Aarti:** around 7 to 8 PM.
- **Shringar Aarti:** around 9 PM.
- **Shayan Aarti:** around 10:30 PM.

Timings shift slightly with the season and on festival days. Check the official site, shrikashivishwanath.org, before you go.

**Tip:** The calmest times are usually late morning on weekdays and after the evening rush. Mondays (Shiva's day), Shivratri, the month of Sawan (usually July to August) and festival days bring enormous queues. If you only want a peaceful first visit, avoid them.

## What You Cannot Take Inside

This is where most foreigners get stuck. The temple has strict security. You cannot take inside:

- Mobile phones
- Cameras
- Smart watches and other electronics, including earbuds and power banks
- Leather belts, wallets and bags
- Shoes and socks
- Cigarettes and lighters

Free or very cheap lockers are available near the gates. You'll leave your phone, bag and shoes there and collect them after.

**Tip:** Go light. Take only your passport, a small amount of cash in your pocket, and nothing else. The less you carry, the faster you get through. Agree with your group in advance exactly where you'll meet afterwards, because you won't have phones inside.

## Dress Code

Cover your shoulders and knees. Western clothes are fine — long trousers or a long skirt and a T-shirt or kurta work perfectly. Avoid shorts, sleeveless tops and very tight or see-through clothing. A light scarf is handy. You'll be barefoot inside, so wear shoes that are easy to take off.

## Sugam Darshan: The Only Safe Way to Skip the Queue

On busy days, general darshan can mean a long wait. The temple offers **Sugam Darshan**, a paid, faster-entry option, which has generally cost around ₹250 to ₹300 per person.

**Book it only through the official website, shrikashivishwanath.org, or the official temple counter.** That's it. Not through your hotel's "contact," not through a man in the lane, and never through someone who approaches you saying "VIP darshan, special entry, no queue."

General darshan is free. You never need to pay anyone to enter the temple.

## The Touts Outside

The lanes around the temple are full of people who want to "help" you. Common lines:

- "Foreigners need a special pass — I can arrange it."
- "Leave your bag at my shop for free" (then you're expected to buy flowers or offerings at inflated prices).
- "I am a temple priest, come with me for special puja."
- "Today the main gate is closed, follow me."

Politely say "no, thank you" and walk to the official entrance and help desk. Using a shop's locker is fine if you're happy to buy a small offering, but agree any price first. I cover the wider pattern in my [Varanasi scams guide](/blog/how-to-avoid-scams-in-varanasi).

## The Corridor: Kashi Vishwanath Dham

The redeveloped Kashi Vishwanath Dham, opened in 2021, transformed the area around the temple. A wide corridor now connects the temple to the river on the Lalita Ghat and Manikarnika side. You can walk from the river up into the complex, which is a beautiful approach, especially in the morning.

The corridor area has open courtyards, smaller shrines, and space to sit. Even if the main queue is long, the complex itself is worth seeing.

## What to Expect Inside

After security and the queue, you'll enter the inner area. The sanctum (garbhagriha) is small, and the Jyotirlinga sits in a silver-plated basin, lower than floor level. It's often crowded and you may only have a few seconds in front of the lingam before temple staff gently move you on.

Devotees offer water, milk, flowers and bel leaves. You can bring a small offering if you like — it's not required. **Sparsh darshan**, where devotees touch the lingam, is often suspended during heavy rush, so don't count on it.

The atmosphere is loud, devotional and intense — chanting, bells, people pressing forward. Don't expect a quiet meditative space. Expect energy.

## Etiquette

- Follow the queue and the instructions of temple staff.
- Don't point your feet at shrines or step over offerings.
- Keep your voice down and don't push.
- Photos are not possible inside anyway, but don't try to sneak a device in.
- Accept prasad (blessed food) with your right hand.
- If a priest offers a blessing and asks for dakshina (an offering), you can give a small amount — ₹20 to ₹100 is plenty — or politely decline.

## Common Mistakes I See Foreigners Make

- **Arriving with a phone and no plan for it.** If you walk up holding your phone, you will be sent back to find a locker, and that is when a "helpful" man appears offering to keep it in his shop for a fee. Leave it at your hotel or use the official lockers near the gate.
- **Wearing shorts "just for the walk there."** You will not be let in. Carry a light scarf or loose trousers in your bag if you are coming straight from the ghats.
- **Forgetting the passport.** A photocopy may not be enough. Carry the original in a zip pouch on your body.
- **Going at peak times.** Mondays, festival days and the Sawan month bring huge queues. A normal weekday morning after 9 AM is far calmer.

**Tip:** If the queue looks endless, don't panic and don't pay a stranger. Walk the corridor to the river viewpoint, have a chai, and come back in an hour. Crowds here move in waves.
