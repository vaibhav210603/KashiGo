# The Perfect 2 Days in Varanasi: A Local's Hour-by-Hour Plan

Most foreigners give Varanasi one rushed day, get overwhelmed by the galis (the narrow lanes), overpay for everything, and leave thinking it was "intense but confusing." That's a shame, because the city rewards a little structure enormously.

I was born near the ghats and have watched thousands of visitors do this wrong. Two full days is the sweet spot: enough to see the river wake up, feel the Aarti, eat properly, and still leave space for the city to surprise you. Here is the plan I give my own friends when they visit.

A quick note on cost, because it's the thing that stresses first-timers most: none of this is expensive if you know the numbers. The whole two days — boats, autos, food, temple visits — should run well under what a single "package tour" quotes you. The trick is simply knowing what each thing costs before someone tells you a made-up price.

## Day 1: The River, the Old City, and the Aarti

### 5:00 AM — Sunrise boat ride

Start on the water. This is the single best experience in Varanasi and it has to be at sunrise, not later. Walk down to Dashashwamedh Ghat or, better, Assi Ghat where boatmen are slightly less aggressive.

A private rowboat for two to four people should cost roughly 600 to 1,200 rupees total for about 1.5 to 2 hours — that is the total price for the whole boat, not per person. Shared boats are 30 to 100 per person. Fix the full price before your foot touches the boat, and never renegotiate once you're on the water. Anyone quoting suspiciously low is planning to change the number mid-river.

Drift from Assi up towards Manikarnika, the main cremation ghat, and watch the city bathe, pray, and begin its day. Say yes to a diya (the little floating lamp) — it's a few rupees and it's lovely.

### 7:30 AM — Breakfast in the lanes

Get off the boat and find kachori sabzi with a side of jalebi. A proper local breakfast costs 40 to 80 rupees. The famous spots near Kachori Gali get busy; anywhere with a crowd of locals eating is a safe bet.

### 9:00 AM — Kashi Vishwanath Temple

The city's most important temple, recently redeveloped into a large corridor complex. Foreigners are welcome. Leave your phone, leather, and bags at the free/cheap lockers outside, dress modestly (shoulders and knees covered), and be prepared for security and queues. Do not pay a "special darshan" tout in the lane outside — book any premium darshan through the official counter, not a stranger.

### 11:00 AM — Wander the old city, then rest

Late morning, lose yourself in the galis around the temple — silk, sweets, tiny shrines, cows, chaos. Then go back to your hotel for the hottest part of the day. Varanasi in the afternoon, especially in summer, is not for sightseeing.

### 6:00 PM — Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh

The evening Ganga Aarti is the city's signature ceremony — fire, bells, chanting, hundreds of people. It starts around 6:00 to 7:30 PM depending on the season, so arrive by 5:30 to get a spot. You have two options: sit on the ghat steps (free, crowded, arrive early) or watch from a small boat on the water (pay per seat, unbeatable view, no jostling). Both are wonderful.

### 8:30 PM — Dinner

End with a thali or, if you want the tourist-favorite, a blue-lassi in the old city and some tandoori. Keep it simple on day one; your stomach is still adjusting.

## Day 2: Slower, Deeper, and a Little Beyond the River

### 5:30 AM — Subah-e-Banaras at Assi Ghat

If day one's evening Aarti felt overwhelming, the morning ceremony at Assi Ghat is its gentle opposite. Subah-e-Banaras is a quiet sunrise ritual — music, yoga, a small aarti — with far fewer people and a completely different, more contemplative energy. Many locals prefer it. It costs nothing to attend.

### 8:00 AM — Breakfast, then Sarnath

After breakfast, take an auto or Uber/Ola out to Sarnath, about 10 to 12 km away, where the Buddha gave his first sermon. It's calm, green, and a total tonal shift from the intensity of the ghats — ancient stupas, a good museum, and space to breathe. A one-way auto should be roughly 150 to 300 rupees; use Uber or Ola to avoid the "tourist price" negotiation entirely. Budget two to three hours including travel.

### 1:00 PM — Lunch and the famous chaat

Back in the city, this is your day for the street food you were warned about but should absolutely try. Tamatar chaat and palak chaat at a busy, well-known stall; a plate is 40 to 100 rupees. Eat where locals queue and where food is fried fresh in front of you.

### 4:00 PM — A second, slower ghat walk

Walk the ghats south to north in the golden late-afternoon light. This is when the ghats are at their most photogenic and least frantic — kids playing cricket, wrestlers training at the akhara, sadhus, laundry drying on the steps. No agenda. Just walk.

### 7:00 PM — Your choice

Repeat the Aarti from a different vantage point, book a final sunset boat, or find a rooftop café overlooking the river. You've earned a slow evening.

## What This Should All Cost

Here's the honest breakdown for two days, per person, excluding your hotel:

Two sunrise/sunset boat rides: roughly 600 to 1,200 total split across your group. Autos and Ubers for the whole trip including Sarnath: 500 to 900. All your food for two days: 400 to 800 if you eat local. Temple entry: free (Kashi Vishwanath), small fees at Sarnath. Diyas, chai, small buys: a few hundred rupees.

In other words, two rich days in Varanasi cost less than a single overpriced "guided package" — if you know the numbers and don't get talked into inflated prices.
