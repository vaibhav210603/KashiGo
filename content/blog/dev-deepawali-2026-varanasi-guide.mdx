# Dev Deepawali 2026 in Varanasi: How to See the Night the Gods Come Down to the Ganga

Every year I get the same message from friends abroad around September: "Is it worth coming to Varanasi for Dev Deepawali?" My answer is always the same. Yes — but only if you plan it properly. Otherwise you'll spend the most beautiful night of the Banarasi year stuck in a crowd behind someone's elbow, unable to see the river.

I was born in the galis (lanes) of Kashi and grew up near Brahma Ghat. Dev Deepawali has been part of every November of my life. This guide is everything I'd tell you over chai if you were planning to come.

## When Is Dev Deepawali 2026?

**Dev Deepawali 2026 falls on Tuesday, 24 November 2026.** It is celebrated on Kartik Purnima, the full moon of the Hindu month of Kartik, about fifteen days after regular Diwali.

The Purnima tithi (lunar day) runs roughly from 11:42 PM on 23 November to about 8:23 PM on 24 November, which is why the main lighting happens on the evening of the 24th. A small number of websites list 25 November. The large majority of panchangs (Hindu almanacs) and local sources say the 24th, but festival dates are fixed by the lunar calendar, so confirm with your hotel or a local contact once you're close to travelling.

**Tip:** Build in a buffer. Arrive in Varanasi by 22 or 23 November and stay until the 25th. If anything shifts, you're covered — and the days around Dev Deepawali are beautiful in their own right.

## What Actually Happens

Dev Deepawali means "Diwali of the gods." The belief is that on Kartik Purnima the devtas (gods) descend to Kashi to bathe in the Ganga and celebrate. The city lights the way for them.

At dusk — roughly 5 PM to 8 PM — lakhs of diyas (small clay oil lamps) are lit along the ghats. From Ravidas Ghat in the south to Rajghat in the north, the whole crescent of the river glows. There are special aartis, fireworks over the water, decorated buildings, and on some ghats, tributes to soldiers who died for the country.

The main Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh becomes a much bigger, grander production than on a normal night. If you've read my [Ganga Aarti guide](/blog/ganga-aarti-varanasi-complete-guide), imagine that, multiplied, with the entire riverfront lit behind it.

Dev Deepawali is also connected with Ganga Mahotsav, a cultural festival of music and dance that the UP tourism department has traditionally run in the days leading up to Kartik Purnima. Dates and format change from year to year, so treat it as a bonus if it's on — check the official UP Tourism announcements closer to the time.

## Book Early. I Mean It.

This is the single most important section in this guide.

- **Hotels:** River-view hotels and guesthouses on or near the ghats book out months ahead and often charge two to four times their normal rate. If you're reading this in October, book today.
- **Boats:** Good boats for the evening are usually gone by the first week of November. Prices run several times normal. Commercial shared "celebration boats" have been advertised at around ₹4,000 per person (approx $45). A private boat for the evening can cost many times its usual hourly rate.
- **Trains and flights:** The days around Kartik Purnima are a busy pilgrimage period. Book transport as early as you can.

**Tip:** If someone at the ghat on the day offers you a "last-minute boat" at a "special price," be very careful. Agree the exact boat, the exact time, the exact duration and the total price — and pay only after you're on board and moving, or pay a small advance at most. ## Where to Watch: Boat vs Ghat Steps vs Rooftop

### From a boat

The classic view. From the river you see the full sweep of lit ghats, which you simply cannot see from the steps. The downside: the river gets genuinely jammed with boats around Dashashwamedh, so you may move very slowly, and you'll pay festival prices. Make sure your boat has life jackets and isn't overloaded. Boats packed far beyond capacity are a real safety issue on this night.

### From the ghat steps

Free, atmospheric, and you're right among the lamps and the people. But near Dashashwamedh the steps fill up by mid-afternoon. If you want to sit, you'll need to claim a spot early and stay there.

### From a rooftop

Some hotels and cafés with river-facing terraces sell tickets or run special dinners for the evening. A good rooftop gives you height, breathing room and a toilet nearby, which matters more than you'd think. Book directly with the property and confirm what's included.

## Which Ghats to Choose

- **Dashashwamedh Ghat:** The main ceremony and the biggest crowd. Spectacular, but the most difficult to move around.
- **Assi Ghat:** The southern end, lively and slightly easier to reach. Good for a relaxed evening with food and cafés nearby.
- **Chet Singh Ghat:** A beautiful old fort-like structure. A calmer place to watch the lamps reflect on the water.
- **Panchganga Ghat:** Further north, with a long tradition of lamp lighting. Quieter and very local.
- **Rajghat:** At the northern end near the Malviya bridge. Often has its own celebrations and gives a different, wider perspective.

My honest suggestion for first-timers: don't try to see everything. Pick one area, settle in, and let the evening come to you.

## Crowd Survival Tips

- **Roads close.** Police restrict vehicles around the ghats on Dev Deepawali, often from the afternoon. Autos and cars will drop you well short — usually around Godowlia or further. Be ready to walk.
- **Arrive by 3 PM** if you want a good spot on the steps. By 5 PM the main areas are packed.
- **Walk from Godowlia.** Even on normal days, vehicles stop at Godowlia and you walk the last 5 to 10 minutes to Dashashwamedh. On festival night that walk can take much longer.
- **Fix a meeting point.** Choose a specific, visible landmark in case you get separated — a named temple or a hotel entrance, not "near the steps."
- **Expect phone signal to jam.** With huge crowds, calls and data often fail. Don't rely on WhatsApp to find each other.
- **Watch for pickpockets.** Front pocket or cross-body bag, zipped. Leave your passport in the hotel safe and carry a photocopy.
- **Kids and elderly:** Think carefully. The crush near Dashashwamedh is intense. A rooftop or a quieter ghat like Chet Singh is far more comfortable. Write your phone number on a card in your child's pocket.
- **Bring water and snacks.** Stalls are overwhelmed and you may be in one spot for hours.

## What to Wear

Late November evenings in Varanasi get cool — often around 13 to 16°C after dark — while afternoons are pleasant. Wear layers, covered shoulders and knees, and comfortable closed shoes you can walk in for a long time. The steps get wet and oily from the lamps, so leave good sandals at home. A light shawl is useful both for warmth and for covering up at temples.

## Festival Scams to Watch For

Festival nights bring out every trick in the book, plus a few extras:

- **Fake boat "bookings"** — someone takes an advance and the boat never shows up, or the "private" boat turns out to have twelve strangers in it.
- **"VIP pass" or "entry ticket" to the ghats** — the ghats are public. There is no ticket to stand on the steps.
- **Forced diya sales** — a child or vendor hands you a lamp, then demands ₹200. A diya usually costs ₹10 to ₹30. Agree the price first.
- **Tilak or blessing fees** — someone marks your forehead and asks for money. Say "no, thank you" before they touch you.
- **Middlemen for rooftops** — book rooftop spots directly with the hotel or café, never through someone on the street.

## Is It Worth It?

Honestly, yes. Dev Deepawali is the one night when the whole of Kashi looks like the city in the stories. But it rewards planning and punishes improvisation. Book your room and your boat early, pick one viewing spot, arrive early, and expect the crowd to be part of the experience.
