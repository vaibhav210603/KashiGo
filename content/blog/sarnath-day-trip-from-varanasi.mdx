# Sarnath Day Trip from Varanasi: A Local's Half-Day Plan

After two days on the ghats — the bells, the boats, the cremation fires, the endless motion — most visitors reach a point where they need to breathe. That's when I send them to Sarnath.

I was born in the galis (lanes) of Kashi, and for me Sarnath was always the quiet place. School trips, family picnics on the lawns, the slow walk around the Dhamek Stupa. It's only about half an hour from the river, but it feels like another world: green, calm, spacious, and deeply peaceful.

It's also where the Buddha gave his first sermon after his enlightenment — one of the four most important pilgrimage sites in Buddhism.

## Sarnath Is Now a UNESCO World Heritage Site

On **25 July 2026**, the "Ancient Buddhist Site, Sarnath" was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List at the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee. It became India's 45th World Heritage Site. The inscribed site includes the Dhamek Stupa, the Dharmarajika Stupa and the Chaukhandi Stupa.

For travellers, this probably means more visitors in the coming seasons, and possibly changes to ticketing and facilities over time. Go early in the day for the calmest experience.

## How Far Is Sarnath from Varanasi?

Sarnath is about 10 to 13 km from the main ghats, depending on where you start. The drive takes roughly 30 to 45 minutes, longer in heavy traffic.

## How to Get There (and What It Costs)

- **Auto-rickshaw:** roughly ₹150 to ₹300 one way (approx $2 to $3.50). Agree the price before you get in. A return trip with waiting time costs more — agree that up front too.
- **Uber or Ola:** roughly ₹250 to ₹450 one way (approx $3 to $5). This is the easiest option for first-timers because the price is fixed and there's no negotiation.
- **Shared autos and e-rickshaws:** cheaper, but involve changes and some local knowledge. Fine if you're adventurous.

**Tip:** Vehicles can't reach the ghats themselves, so walk up to Godowlia or another main road to book your ride. Coming back, apps work well from Sarnath, though at quieter times you may wait a few minutes for a driver.

Watch out for the classic detour: a driver who says "Sarnath is closed today" or suggests stopping at a "silk factory" on the way. Say "no shops, please" before you set off. I explain why in my [Varanasi scams guide](/blog/how-to-avoid-scams-in-varanasi).

## What to See

### Dhamek Stupa

The big one. A massive, solid stupa about 43 metres high, standing on the spot traditionally associated with the Buddha's first sermon. The lower part has beautiful carved stonework. Walk around it clockwise, as pilgrims do. You'll often see monks from Sri Lanka, Thailand, Tibet and elsewhere chanting at its base.

### The excavated ruins and Dharmarajika Stupa

Around the Dhamek Stupa are the brick foundations of ancient monasteries and the remains of the Dharmarajika Stupa, believed to have been built by Emperor Ashoka. You'll also see the broken base of the Ashokan pillar, protected under a small enclosure.

### Archaeological Museum

This is the one place you should not skip. The star is the **Ashoka Lion Capital** — four lions back to back — which is India's national emblem. You've seen it on every rupee note; here's the real thing. The museum also has one of the finest seated Buddha sculptures in India.

**The museum is closed on Fridays.** It's usually open from about 9:30 AM to 5 PM on other days. Photography inside is generally not allowed and bags may need to be left at the cloakroom.

### Mulagandha Kuti Vihara

A modern temple built by the Maha Bodhi Society, with striking murals of the Buddha's life painted by a Japanese artist. Next to it is a Bodhi tree that is said to have grown from a cutting connected to the original tree in Bodh Gaya.

### Deer Park

The Buddha's first sermon is said to have taken place in a deer park, and there's still a small deer enclosure. It's simple and nice if you're with kids.

### International monasteries

Thai, Tibetan, Japanese, Chinese and other Buddhist temples are scattered around Sarnath, each built in its own national style. The Thai temple and its large standing Buddha statue are particularly worth a short walk.

### Chaukhandi Stupa

On the road in from Varanasi, a little before the main site. A tall brick mound topped by an octagonal Mughal-era tower. It marks the place where the Buddha is said to have met his first five disciples. Ask your driver to stop for 15 minutes.

## Tickets

Ticket prices change, so treat these as approximate and check at the counter:

- **Main excavated site (ASI):** around ₹300 for foreign nationals, much less for Indian citizens.
- **Archaeological Museum:** a separate, small ticket — roughly ₹100 for foreigners.
- **Temples and monasteries:** generally free, though donations are welcome.

Card or UPI payment is often accepted at ASI counters, but carry some cash just in case.

## A Simple Half-Day Plan

- **8:30 AM:** Leave Varanasi after an early breakfast.
- **9:00 AM:** Quick stop at Chaukhandi Stupa.
- **9:30 AM:** Archaeological Museum, opening time, before the groups arrive.
- **10:30 AM:** Dhamek Stupa and the excavated ruins.
- **11:30 AM:** Mulagandha Kuti Vihara, Deer Park and one or two international temples.
- **12:30 PM:** Lunch, then head back to Varanasi.

That's about four hours door to door. If you go on a Friday, skip the museum and swap in more time at the temples — but honestly, the museum is worth planning around.

## Food

Sarnath is quieter than the old city, with simple restaurants and dhabas near the main site serving thalis, North Indian staples and some Tibetan food like momos and thukpa. Expect to pay roughly ₹150 to ₹400 per person for a simple meal. Carry water, especially in the hot months.

## What to Wear and Etiquette

Modest clothes — covered shoulders and knees — are respectful at all the Buddhist temples. Remove shoes before entering temple halls. Walk clockwise around stupas. Keep your voice low near meditating monks and pilgrims, and ask before photographing people.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- **Going on a Friday and expecting the museum.** The stupas and park are open, but the Archaeological Museum is closed, and the Lion Capital is the one thing you should not miss.
- **Agreeing to a "Sarnath package" from a ghat tout.** These often include a long stop at a silk "factory" on the way. Book an Uber or Ola, or agree a return auto price with waiting time before you leave.
- **Arriving at midday in April to June.** There is very little shade around the ruins. Go early, or after 3 PM in the hot months.
- **Buying "antique" Buddha statues near the gate.** They are new, and genuine antiquities cannot legally be exported from India anyway.

**Tip:** Ask your auto driver to wait at the main gate rather than paying him to "guide" you. A waiting fee of a couple of hundred rupees is normal; agree it upfront.
