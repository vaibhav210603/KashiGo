# When Should You Actually Visit Varanasi? An Honest Local Guide

The short answer every travel blog gives you is "October to March." It's not wrong, but it hides a lot. Some of those months are magical and some are freezing and foggy; some cheaper months are genuinely fine if you know what you're getting into.

I've lived by the ghats my whole life and seen the city in every season. Here's the honest version — what each part of the year is actually like for a foreign visitor, so you can match your trip to what you want.

## The Quick Answer

If you want the best overall experience and don't mind crowds, come between late October and February. If you want the single most spectacular moment of the year, come for Dev Deepawali in November. If you're on a budget and can handle heat, the shoulder months are cheaper and emptier. Avoid the peak of the monsoon (July to mid-September) unless you have a specific reason.

Now the detail.

## October to February: Peak Season (and Why)

This is the window everyone recommends, and for good reason. Daytime temperatures sit in a comfortable range, mornings are cool and atmospheric, and the light on the river is soft and golden — perfect for sunrise boats and photography.

The trade-offs: this is also when prices for hotels and boats are highest, the ghats are busiest, and December-January mornings can be genuinely cold with thick fog that occasionally delays trains and flights. Pack a warm layer for early boat rides — first-timers are always surprised that Varanasi can be cold.

Within this window, **November** deserves a special mention because of Dev Deepawali. About fifteen days after Diwali, the entire sweep of ghats is lit with hundreds of thousands of oil lamps in one of the most breathtaking sights in India. If you can only come once, and you can handle serious crowds, come for this. Book your accommodation months ahead.

## March to Early April: The Underrated Shoulder

March is quietly one of the best times to visit and almost nobody tells you this. The winter fog is gone, the weather is warm but not yet brutal, the peak crowds have thinned, and prices soften. Holi, the festival of colours, falls in March and Varanasi celebrates it with famous intensity — joyful, chaotic, and not for the faint-hearted, but unforgettable.

By April it's heating up fast, and you'll want to be off the ghats by mid-morning.

## Mid-April to June: The Hot Season

Let's be honest: it's hot. Really hot. Daytime temperatures can climb past 40°C and the middle of the day is oppressive. This is the cheapest and emptiest time to visit, and if you structure your days around dawn and dusk — sunrise boats, indoor rest through the afternoon heat, evening Aarti — it's entirely doable. You'll have the ghats far more to yourself.

This season suits budget travelers and anyone who genuinely dislikes crowds more than they dislike heat. Hydrate constantly, and don't try to sightsee at noon.

## July to Mid-September: Monsoon (Approach With Caution)

The rains bring the Ganga up, sometimes dramatically. In heavy years the river floods the lower ghats, boat rides get restricted or suspended for safety, and the famous ghat-side walkways can be partly underwater. The city is green and dramatic and far cheaper, but you're gambling with the exact experiences most people come for.

If you visit now, keep your plans flexible, don't book non-refundable boat experiences far ahead, and check the river's status close to your dates. This is the one window I'd steer a first-timer away from unless the dates are fixed for other reasons.

## Late September to October: The Reopening

As the monsoon retreats, the river settles, boats return to normal, and the weather becomes pleasant again before the peak-season crowds and prices arrive. Late September and early October can be a lovely, good-value window — one of my personal favourites.

## Matching the Season to What Everything Costs

Here's the part that matters for your wallet: your season affects the *starting* price a boatman or hotel quotes you, but it does not change the *fair* price of a boat ride, an auto, or a plate of chaat. In peak season, the opening quote you'll hear is simply higher and pushier — which means knowing the real numbers matters even more.

A sunrise rowboat is a fair 600 to 1,200 rupees total whether it's a quiet March morning or a packed November evening. The difference is only in how hard someone tries to talk you past that number.
